# ADR 0003 — Lead-Gen Pivot: Actor Identity, Drafting Decoupled, Statewide, No Revenue Gate

**Status:** Accepted (Captain decision, 2026-05-07, audit conversation on contractor mis-classification).

> **Context note (2026-07-03).** The automated lead-gen machine these rules governed was retired
> wholesale by [ADR 0060](./0060-retire-automated-lead-gen-machine.md) (2026-07-01), which
> deliberately did not supersede this ADR. The mechanism-specific rules (pipeline filters, machine
> drafting posture) are moot with the machine gone; the principles — actor identity over
> permit-filer identity, no revenue-band gate, no fabricated pre-knowledge of a prospect's
> business — remain venture doctrine for manual and Operator-drafted outreach.

**Source:** Audit on 2026-05-07 walked through the `/admin/entities` Signal queue. Five of the six top rows were licensed contractors (sign companies, general contractors) being treated as the operating business that filed the permit. Outreach drafts narrated each prospect's business state and offered a fabricated "one-page breakdown." A hardcoded $750k–$5M revenue gate and Phoenix-only geography filter excluded prospects we'd actually take.

---

## Context

The lead-gen system, as architected through 2026-Q2, surfaces wrong-actor prospects, fabricates customer-facing copy at qualification time, and applies pre-launch filters (revenue range, geography) that exclude real opportunities. Each of these is a structural failure, not a copy problem.

**Wrong-actor disease.** The Phoenix permit dataset's `PROFESS_NAME` field is the licensed contractor's company. The Mesa dataset exposes `applicant` and `application_name` ambiguously. The Tempe dataset exposes only `ProjectName`. None of these datasets carry a field that reliably identifies the operating business at the permitted address. The schema-level resolver in `workers/new-business/src/soda.ts` already tags every record `'business' | 'contractor' | 'unknown'` — but the role tag is dropped before reaching the qualification step. The pipeline literally does not know which records are the contractor versus the business.

**Pattern A leak through `outreach_angle`.** All three qualification prompts (`new-business`, `job-monitor`, `review-mining`) include `outreach_angle` as a required output. The angle is generated by an LLM at qualification time, before any per-prospect enrichment runs, and renders directly on the entity row in admin. With nothing but a permit address and a contractor name, the model produces sentences like _"You've got the foundation most sign companies spend years trying to build"_ — claims about the prospect's internal business state grounded in nothing. This violates `feedback_no_pretend_to_know_business.md` (positioning law: collaborator, not condescending tech-bro).

**Pattern B leak through prompt-baked deliverables.** The outreach prompt instructs the model to _"offer to send them something specific. A one-page breakdown of what other companies their size have done."_ — a deliverable we do not ship. Every cold draft promises an artifact that doesn't exist.

**Artificial revenue gate.** Both `new-business-prompt.ts` and `job-qualification-prompt.ts` hardcode `$750k–$5M revenue` as the target buy box, and `DEFAULT_REVENUE_RANGE` lives in `generator/types.ts`. None of the data sources expose revenue; the size estimate is a heuristic guess. Filtering on a guessed estimate of an unverifiable field is artificial. Pre-revenue, "too many qualified leads" is a good problem to have.

**Phoenix-only geography.** SerpAPI location string `'Phoenix, Arizona, United States'`, Outscraper geo bias 50 km from downtown Phoenix, query strings suffixed with `Phoenix AZ` / `Scottsdale AZ`, and the `geos: ['Phoenix metro, AZ']` config field — every layer in the lead-gen stack carries an unnecessary metro-only assumption. Most consulting work runs remotely; in-person travel anywhere in Arizona is feasible for the right engagement.

**Permits-as-trigger is the wrong unit of analysis.** Even when the actor is correctly identified, a single commercial permit is a weak signal of "growing business." Most permits are routine work. The strongest signals in the available stack are first-party pain articulations: specific operational role hires (job_monitor) and clustered operational complaints in reviews (review_mining). Permits are useful as enrichment for entities already discovered, not as triggers for new ones.

## Decision

The lead-gen pipeline is restructured around actor identity, with drafting decoupled from qualification, no revenue gate, and statewide reach. Seven specific decisions, all interlocking:

### 1. Actor identity is a first-class concept

Every ingest record carries `actor_role: 'business' | 'contractor' | 'unknown'` from the source resolver through to qualification. For `'business'` records (Scottsdale licenses), the path is unchanged. For `'contractor'` and `'unknown'` records, the record is **not** qualified as a prospect — it routes to address-based recovery (decision #3) or is dropped.

The role is computed at the source-schema level by `resolveBusinessName()` and propagated through `PermitRecord.actor_role` into the worker run loop.

### 2. Permits are enrichment, not triggers

A permit creates no new entity. Permits attach as `permit_observed` context entries to entities discovered through real-business-identifying sources: ADOR TPT licenses (when feasible), ACC corporate filings, Scottsdale licenses, review-mined businesses, job-monitored companies. The address is the join key.

Triggers stay with the sources that actually identify operating businesses by their own activity. Permits earn their place as evidence on those entities, not as standalone signals.

### 3. Three-tier ingest-time recovery for ambiguous-actor permits

For `actor_role !== 'business'` records, the worker runs three recovery tiers sequentially at ingest:

- **Tier 1 (internal address+name match).** Look up existing entities by normalized address. Address match alone is insufficient — multi-tenant addresses (strip mall, office complex) would mis-attribute the sushi restaurant's permit to the chiropractor next door. Auto-append the `permit_observed` context entry only when address match AND a soft name/owner match. Multi-candidate addresses or name mismatches log to `candidate_merge_log` for human review and bail out.
- **Tier 2 (ADOR TPT lookup).** Stubbed behind a feature flag; returns `null` until ADOR bulk feasibility lands. The slot is reserved.
- **Tier 3 (Google Places reverse lookup).** Gated to occupancy-implying permit types (TI, change of occupancy, commercial remodel) and capped by a weekly budget. On hit with a name consistent with any existing entity at the address, join. On hit with a brand-new business, create the entity. On no hit or business status not OPERATIONAL, drop.

On total miss across all three tiers, the permit is dropped at ingest with `summary.droppedOrphan++`. **No standing orphan lane.** Permits filed before their matching business is discovered are unrecoverable retroactively. This is an accepted trade-off (Captain decision: drop at ingest, monitor the orphan rate via telemetry, revisit only if the rate runs high).

### 4. Drafting is decoupled from qualification

`outreach_angle` is removed from every qualification output and validator. Workers stop writing it to context metadata. The entity row view renders two factual layers:

1. **Signal evidence** (always shown): composed from existing fields — e.g., `New business license · "Sonoran Desert Plumbing LLC" · Scottsdale · 2026-05-03`.
2. **Enrichment summary first sentence** (when available): drawn from existing `dispatchEnrichmentWorkflow` context, which runs after entity create. Descriptive, sourced from public web data, never LLM-narrated.

Outreach drafting moves to `src/lib/claude/outreach.ts` and runs only after Captain promotes an entity to Prospect stage and enrichment context is assembled. This is the only layer in the system that produces customer-facing copy — and it now runs through a two-stage Pattern A validator (decision #7).

### 5. No revenue/size gate at the lead-gen layer

`DEFAULT_REVENUE_RANGE`, the `RevenueRange` type, and the `revenue_range` config field on every pipeline config interface are removed from `generator/types.ts`. The `$750k–$5M revenue` framing is removed from all three qualification prompts. The `size_estimate` field stays in qualification output as **telemetry only** — preserved in metadata, never gates a decision.

Structural disqualifications remain. The list is now five items:

- National-chain franchise (corporate playbook, owner has no autonomy)
- Government / school district / municipal (procurement / mission mismatch)
- Non-profit / religious organization (different operating model)
- Holding / shell / single-member-investment LLC (no operations to consult on)
- Enterprise / 500+ employees / multi-state corporate buyer (procurement-style buyer pattern, not owner-led)

The enterprise filter is re-categorized from "size filter" to "structural filter" — the mechanism that made it useful (procurement vs. owner buyer pattern) was always structural, not size-based. Without it, the statewide pivot would surface hundreds of Banner Health, Honeywell, and Intel postings per week.

### 6. Statewide geography at the lead-gen layer

Phoenix-bias removed from every layer:

- `job_monitor` SerpAPI location → `'Arizona, United States'`.
- `review_mining` geo bias → state-level (or `locationBias` removed entirely).
- `review_mining` discovery queries → drop `Phoenix AZ` / `Scottsdale AZ` suffixes; replace with `Arizona` or no city qualifier; add diversification queries for underserved verticals (machine shop, MSP, marketing agency, commercial electrical).
- All prompt language → `Arizona-based` replaces `Phoenix-area` / `Phoenix-based`.
- `geos` config field → `['Arizona']` (kept for documentation, not used to filter).

City-level SODA fetchers (Phoenix, Scottsdale, Mesa, Tempe) stay as-is for now. New city sources are follow-on. State-level sources (TPT, ACC, ROC) — when added — subsume most of the city-expansion work.

Marketing copy / `CLAUDE.md` positioning text update is a **separate Captain decision** and is not gated by this work.

### 7. Two-stage Pattern A validator

Every outreach draft runs through both stages before save:

- **Stage 1 (mechanical pre-filter).** Em dash, first-person voice (`I`, `I'm`, etc.), word count > 120, banlist of ~25 phrases drawn from `feedback_no_pretend_to_know_business.md` and the audited failing drafts. Catches obvious violations cheaply.
- **Stage 2 (structural classifier).** A single Haiku call: _"Does this draft make any claim about the recipient's business state, history, growth phase, internal experience, or what they're feeling/thinking that wasn't supplied in the input intelligence? Answer Y or N, and quote the offending phrase if Y."_ This is the actual rule. The classifier is the merge gate; the banlist is the fast path.

A banlist alone is whack-a-mole — the LLM routes around enumerated phrases. The classifier is grounded in the rule itself, not in a list of historical violations. On either stage failure, the draft is not saved; the violation surfaces in the admin UI with the classifier's quoted phrase as feedback.

### 8. Cross-generator dedup is log-only

`computeSlug` is hardened (lowercase, suffix stripping, normalization). Jaro-Winkler fuzzy match against same-area entities runs on every create; near-misses log to `candidate_merge_log` for human review. **No auto-merge.** The threshold is an admin-tunable `pipeline_settings` config value (default 0.92, range 0.8–0.99).

Address-based dedup applies symmetrically: when a Tier-3 Places lookup discovers a business at an address where we already have an entity, prefer joining only if name signals are consistent.

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative                                                    | Rejected because                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Keep contractor-tagged records as prospects                    | The audit confirmed five of six visible Signal rows were contractors. This is the disease, not a debate.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Drop permits entirely from the pipeline                        | Permits carry useful information about a known prospect's address (operational changes, capital deployment). As enrichment, they earn their place. As triggers, they don't.                                                                                                                   |
| Maintain a standing `permit_address_index` lane                | Contradicts Captain's "drop at ingest, no orphan lane" directive. Carrying cost on storage, queries, and admin UI for prospects we cannot reach today. Permits filed before their matching business is discovered are an accepted trade-off — if orphan rate runs high in telemetry, revisit. |
| Banlist alone for Pattern A enforcement                        | The LLM will route around enumerated phrases. The structural classifier is the actual rule.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Keep the $750k–$5M revenue gate as a pre-launch sanity limiter | Pre-revenue venture has no conversion data. Reducing volume before measuring conversion is premature optimization. "Too many qualified leads that convert" is a good problem to have. Size stays as telemetry.                                                                                |
| Phoenix-metro-only geography                                   | Most consulting work is remote. Captain is willing to travel anywhere in Arizona for the right engagement. Artificial geography filters exclude real prospects.                                                                                                                               |
| Auto-merge entities above a fuzzy threshold                    | False-positive merges destroy data and erode trust. Log-only with human review is the safer first step. Calibration of the threshold is empirical and runs against logged candidate pairs after the pivot has been live.                                                                      |
| Pivot to inbox UX (instead of queue)                           | Captain rejected. Volume is unprocessed because the processing pipeline isn't built, not because there's too much. Queue stays.                                                                                                                                                               |

## Consequences

### Telemetry counters introduced

Run-summary JSON for each generator extends with the following counters:

- `droppedByRole` (new_business): contractor or unknown role records routed to recovery
- `droppedByActorRole` (job_monitor): staffing-agency, syndicator, or unknown postings rejected
- `droppedOrphan` (new_business): permits where all three recovery tiers missed
- `budgetSkipped` (new_business): Tier-3 Places lookup skipped due to weekly budget cap
- `recoveredTier1` / `recoveredTier3` (new_business): how many ambiguous permits were successfully attached to a real entity at each tier

### New tables

- `places_lookup_cache` — TTL-bounded cache for Google Places reverse-address lookups. Cache hits do not count against the weekly budget.
- `candidate_merge_log` — audit trail for fuzzy-match near-misses and address+name ambiguities. Human-review surface for the admin UI in a follow-on.

### Admin-tunable settings (per pipeline)

- `pain_threshold` (review_mining, job_monitor, new_business): per-pipeline qualification threshold.
- `weekly_places_budget_usd` (new_business): Tier-3 reverse-lookup budget guard.
- `dedup_fuzzy_threshold` (new_business): Jaro-Winkler threshold for logging near-miss duplicates.

### Pattern A enforcement on every customer-facing draft

Every draft runs the two-stage validator. Violations do not save. The classifier-quoted phrase becomes the retry feedback.

### Out of scope — follow-on epics

These were explicitly deferred (Captain decision, option (c) in the planning conversation):

- **Reprocess the existing 270 Signal-stage entities** through the new pipeline.
- **End-to-end conversion telemetry** (signal → assessment booked → engagement signed). This is the only metric that tells us which generator actually produces revenue; the current architecture is flying blind on outcomes.
- **ADOR TPT bulk feasibility investigation.** Tier-2 stays stubbed until this lands. State-wide TPT licenses are the single best business-at-address dataset in Arizona; if accessible, this is the biggest single win for the recovery pipeline.
- **Threshold calibration exercise for `dedup_fuzzy_threshold`.** Run after the pivot has been live a week, against logged `candidate_merge_log` rows at the default 0.92. Captain spot-reviews ~30 pairs at each candidate threshold (0.88, 0.90, 0.92, 0.95) to pick the best precision/recall trade.
- **New city / state SODA fetchers** beyond what we have today. State-level sources (TPT, ACC, ROC) subsume most of this once feasibility lands.
- **Marketing copy / `CLAUDE.md` positioning update** for statewide reach. Separate Captain decision.
- **Admin UI rework** for the new generator config shape (minimal config edits ride along with this pivot; full rework is follow-on).

### Architectural relationship to ADR 0001

ADR 0001 established the two-layer taxonomy (5-cat observation, 6-cat delivery). This ADR builds on that foundation: the lead-gen pipeline speaks observation language, attaches it to entities the marketing site speaks delivery language to, and the assessment conversation is the bridge. ADR 0001 is unchanged.

### Risks accepted

- **Orphan-permit unrecoverability.** Permits filed today for businesses we don't yet know about cannot be retroactively joined. Accepted trade-off; revisit only if telemetry shows a high orphan rate.
- **Pattern A classifier cost.** One Haiku call per draft (~$0.001). Negligible relative to the draft generation itself.
- **Multi-tenant address ambiguity.** Some permits at multi-tenant addresses will route to `candidate_merge_log` rather than auto-attaching. Human review burden is real but bounded.
- **Statewide SerpAPI cost.** Plan keeps query count constant (queries are statewide-flavored, not multiplied by city). If volume blows the $50/mo plan, reduce query list rather than removing geo expansion.
