## The prior lumbar episode, May and June 2021

### Visit 1: May 12, 2021

**Chief complaint:** Low back pain.

**History of present illness.** Patient is a 34-year-old female presenting with low back pain that began yesterday afternoon at work. She reports she lifted a case of product from a floor pallet and felt a "pull" across her lower back. Pain is across the belt line, described as an aching soreness, rated 5 of 10, worse with bending and with getting out of a chair, better with lying down. She reports she was able to finish her shift.

She denies pain radiating into either leg. Denies numbness or tingling. Denies weakness. Denies bowel or bladder change. Denies fever, chills, or night sweats. Denies unexplained weight loss. Denies prior back injury of any kind.

She took over-the-counter ibuprofen last night with partial relief.

**Examination.** Vitals within normal limits. Ambulates without difficulty, mild guarding rising from the chair. Lumbar paraspinal tenderness at approximately L4 through S1, midline non-tender, no step-off. Mild paraspinal muscle tightness, right greater than left. Lumbar flexion mildly limited by discomfort, extension full. **Straight leg raise negative bilaterally.** Motor 5 of 5 in bilateral lower extremities. Sensation intact. Deep tendon reflexes 2+ and symmetric at the patellae and Achilles. Gait normal.

**Assessment.** Acute lumbar strain, mechanical, without radicular features. No red flags.

**Plan.** No imaging indicated. Naproxen 500 mg twice daily with food for ten days. Cyclobenzaprine 5 mg at bedtime as needed for five days. Heat. Remain active as tolerated; avoid bed rest. Home exercise handout provided and reviewed. Lifting restriction at work, no lifting over 20 pounds for two weeks; work note provided. Return in two weeks or sooner if symptoms worsen. Red flag precautions reviewed.

**Diagnosis:** Strain of lumbar region, initial encounter (S39.012A)

### Visit 2: May 26, 2021

**Chief complaint:** Follow-up, low back pain.

**Subjective.** Patient reports substantial improvement. Pain now 2 of 10, present mainly in the morning and after prolonged sitting. She has been doing the home exercises. She has been working with the lifting restriction and reports no difficulty. She stopped the cyclobenzaprine after four days because it made her drowsy. She continues naproxen as needed.

Still denies any leg symptoms, numbness, tingling, or weakness.

**Examination.** Minimal residual lumbar paraspinal tenderness. No spasm appreciated today. Lumbar range of motion full in flexion and extension. Straight leg raise negative bilaterally. Neurologically intact. Gait normal.

**Assessment.** Lumbar strain, improving as expected.

**Plan.** Continue home exercise program. Naproxen as needed. Advance lifting restriction to 35 pounds for the next four weeks. Work note provided. Return in approximately four weeks for release.

### Visit 3: June 23, 2021

**Chief complaint:** Follow-up, low back pain, for release.

**Subjective.** Patient reports she is **back to normal**. She denies pain today and reports she has had no back pain in approximately two weeks. She has resumed all activity at home and at work, including lifting, without difficulty. She has stopped all medication.

**Examination.** No lumbar tenderness. No spasm. Full and painless lumbar range of motion in all planes. Straight leg raise negative bilaterally. Motor 5 of 5. Sensation intact. Reflexes 2+ and symmetric. Gait normal.

**Assessment.** **Lumbar strain, resolved.**

**Plan.** **Discharged from care for this complaint. Released to full duty with no restrictions.** Continue the home exercise program as a general measure. Return as needed if symptoms recur.

**Total course of care: three office visits over six weeks, May 12, 2021 to June 23, 2021.**

**No imaging obtained at any point. No injections. No physical therapy. No specialist referral. No restrictions at discharge.**

## Notes for the file

**Why this matters.** The 2021 episode is the fact the defense will lead with on causation. It is disclosed and it is real. It is also narrow.

The disclosure chain is clean. Ms. Alvarez volunteered the 2021 episode at the emergency department on the night of the collision, before any lawyer was involved, and it was taken again in detail by Dr. Castellanos at the initial orthopedic consultation on May 31, 2024. The history she gave both times matches what this production shows. Nothing in the prior record contradicts anything she has reported.

**The scope point.** The prior episode was lumbar, mechanical, non-radicular, and resolved in six weeks with three office visits and no imaging. The injury claimed here includes a cervical injury with an MRI-correlated C5-C6 disc protrusion in the symptomatic distribution, and the prior record contains no cervical history whatsoever.

**The interval point.** Thirty-five months separate the 2021 discharge from the collision, and that interval includes three annual examinations at which the musculoskeletal review of systems was negative.

**The lumbar point.** For the lumbar spine, the prior episode is a genuine element for the defense to argue. The comparison that answers it is in the records: 2021 was three visits, no imaging, no injections, negative straight leg raise throughout, resolved in six weeks. 2024 was eight physical therapy sessions, an MRI showing an L4-L5 annular fissure, trigger point injections, an epidural steroid injection under fluoroscopy, positive straight leg raise on the left across four examinations, and seven months of care.

**Related items.** No expert has been retained or designated to address causation or apportionment, and no apportionment opinion appears anywhere in the file.
