## Working alongside your team and your systems

An Operator is a team member that works inside the systems you already run, not another program your staff has to learn. A few things hold true across everything it does:

- It connects to Smokeball and the tools around it, and works from your matters, documents, tasks, and calendar the way your staff does.
- It keeps track of what's happening on a matter: what's due and what's still open.
- It does the routine carrying work and brings a person the result already prepared, so their part is usually a quick review or a single approval.
- When the rest doesn't need anyone, it holds it in one short summary instead of a stream of messages.
- Where a careful employee would check with you instead of guessing, it checks with you. Those moments are called out below, not buried.
- Anything that goes to another party or to the court goes to an attorney first.

You set how far it goes on its own. For each kind of work, you decide whether it just handles it or prepares it and brings it to a person. Some things you'll want it to take care of; others you'll want eyes on every time. Start it cautious and give it more room as it earns trust, the way you would with a new hire. It's your dial, and you can change it whenever.

It writes in your firm's voice. We set it up from your own letters and templates, so the drafts it prepares, a meet-and-confer letter, a verification request, read like your office wrote them rather than like they came from a tool.

It also tunes as it goes. It picks up your firm's patterns and preferences from the work, and when someone corrects it, a date, a document type, a draft, it carries that correction forward. Tuning isn't a one-time setup we finish and leave behind. Getting more accurate on your documents and your way of working is part of how the Operator runs.

Two things let it keep up without anyone having to watch it:

- It watches wherever the work lives. It can have its own inbox, monitor another inbox, a shared folder, or another system you point it at, or take what your team forwards. Anything routed to it reaches it the moment it arrives. Anything it watches, it checks on a schedule.
- It runs on schedules you set. It can run as many scheduled jobs as the work needs, at whatever frequency fits, checking your open matters for what's due, what's outstanding, and what needs a nudge.

Each task below is its own routine, so when one of these comes up, it knows what to do.

## Discovery

You said discovery is where the most falls through, so this is where we go deepest.

**Getting discovery to the Operator.** Discovery comes in by mail and email before it's filed into Smokeball, and you told us closing that gap matters most. The Operator can take discovery in whatever way fits how you work. It can watch a shared inbox or a mailbox you point it at, take what your team forwards, or pick it up from wherever you route it. We'd set that up with you. The one honest limit is the same as for any new hire: a person still has to open and scan paper mail before anyone, or anything, can read it.

_How it works: the inbox it watches is the trigger. It reads the message and its attachments, recognizes a served-discovery document from the contents (the caption, the document title, the requests themselves), matches it to the right Smokeball matter by case name and number, and opens that matter through the connector to work from._

**Reading it and getting the deadline on the calendar.** The Operator reads a served document, identifies the type (interrogatories, requests for production, requests for admission, a deposition notice), and pulls the service date and method from the proof of service. From that it works out the response deadline, puts it on the Smokeball calendar and as a task on the matter, and asks the responsible attorney to confirm before it's treated as final. One question for us to settle together: do you already use Smokeball's court-rules calendaring, the one tied to InfoTrack, to compute these dates? If so, the Operator uses that engine and confirms the result. If they're figured by hand today, the Operator can compute them. Either way it lands on the calendar and gets confirmed before anyone relies on it. We'd tune the reading on your real documents, and it keeps sharpening as it's corrected, so you can trust the type and the service date it pulls before you lean on it.

_How it works: it finds the proof of service in the document and reads the service date and method. A deadline routine applies the California response window for that discovery type and service method, then writes a calendar event and a matter task back through the Smokeball connector, flagged for the attorney to confirm. The date logic is what we calibrate on your matters, or hand to Smokeball's court-rules engine if you run it._

**Client verification.** You said this is the piece that slips most often. The Operator can own it start to finish: prepare the verification, send it to the client through Smokeball e-sign, track it as an open item, and follow up with the client on a steady cadence until it's signed. If you'd rather collect verifications another way, it works with that instead.

_How it works: when a response is ready, a verification routine creates the request, sends it through Smokeball e-sign, and logs it as an open task on the matter. A scheduled job re-checks the matter on the cadence you set, reads from Smokeball whether the signature is back, and reminds the client again if it isn't, until it's signed._

**The separate statement.** You flagged this as a real time sink. The Operator can assemble the item-by-item separate statement that pairs each request with its response and the stated grounds, ready for an attorney to finalize.

_How it works: it reads the served requests and your drafted responses from the matter, then a separate-statement routine lays them into the item-by-item table California requires, each request next to its response and the stated grounds, formatted for an attorney to finalize._

**Discovery you propound.** You asked us to track the discovery the firm sends out, not just what comes in. The Operator watches those response deadlines, and when the other side runs late or answers thinly, it raises it: time to meet and confer, and the window to move to compel. You told us meet and confer is sometimes handled informally first, so the Operator brings that decision to the attorney rather than sending a letter on its own.

_How it works: it records the response date when you serve, and a scheduled job watches that date across your open matters. When a response is late or comes back thin, it flags the meet-and-confer point and starts the motion-to-compel clock, and brings the decision to the attorney._

## Medical records and the chronology

You named keeping the medical chronology current as new records arrive as one of the heavier ongoing burdens. Records come in through YoCierge straight into the Smokeball matter, and they keep coming through the life of the case. The Operator can watch for new records, keep a running chronology and treatment timeline current as they land, and chase the records still outstanding. That chronology feeds your demands, your mediation briefs, and your valuations, so it's ready when you need it instead of rebuilt each time.

_How it works: a scheduled job checks the matter for new records arriving from YoCierge through Smokeball. When a set lands, it reads them, pulls the dates, providers, diagnoses, and treatment, and adds them to a running chronology it keeps on the matter._

## Case initiation and the complaint

When a matter opens, the Operator can set up the case: the folder structure, the standard tasks for that matter type, and the deadlines already in view, including the statute of limitations and the service deadlines for each defendant. It can assemble the filing package and stage it for the venue you're filing in. When a service confirmation comes back through InfoTrack, it can pick that up and start the responsive-pleading clock.

_How it works: when a matter opens, a setup routine creates the standard folders and tasks for that matter type through the connector. For service, it watches for InfoTrack's confirmation to post to the matter, reads the served date, and writes the responsive-pleading deadline as a calendar event and task._

## Motions

The Operator can track the motion calendar for each matter: what's filed, what's due, the hearing dates. For your motions, it can assemble the package and stage it for filing, with the format and procedural requirements for the court and department it's going to. Where a court requires a reserved hearing date, it can stage that. Where a court posts tentative rulings, it can watch for them so the team sees them in time. The drafting itself stays with your drafting tools. The Operator prepares their inputs and routes their output. Which tool drafts what, between BriefPoint, CoCounsel, and Claude, is the piece we settle after your Friday meeting, so there's no overlap and no wasted cost.

_How it works: a scheduled job reads each matter's motion deadlines and hearing dates and keeps them current. When you're filing, an assembly routine pulls the documents from the matter and stages the package to the court and department's format, ready for the attorney and for filing through InfoTrack._

## Minor's compromise

You said this is a significant part of your practice, so it's built in. Once a matter is headed for a minor's compromise, the Operator can set up that track: assemble the petition packet with the MC-350 and MC-351 forms, track the guardian ad litem appointment, and watch the hearing date. It can pull together what the disclosure requires, the settlement breakdown, the fees, the costs, and the liens, so the net to the minor is clear, and chase the lien figures it needs. It can surface how the minor's funds have to be handled after approval, usually a blocked account, and track the steps through funding.

_How it works: an assembly routine pulls the settlement, fee, cost, and lien figures from the matter and fills the MC-350 and MC-351 forms for the attorney to finalize. A scheduled job tracks the guardian ad litem appointment and hearing date and chases the lien payoff figures the disclosure needs._

## Trial preparation

As a matter heads to trial, the Operator can build the trial binder: the exhibit list, the witness list, the deposition summaries, and the exhibits assembled and Bates-stamped in your PDF tools. It tracks the trial-prep deadlines and pre-trial filings, so nothing surfaces for the first time the week before trial.

_How it works: an assembly routine pulls the exhibits, deposition summaries, and lists from the matter and organizes the binder, exhibits Bates-stamped through your PDF tools. A scheduled job keeps the trial-prep and pre-trial-filing deadlines current on the matter._

## Mediation and settlement

For a mediation or settlement conference, the Operator can assemble the brief inputs and track the deadlines, including a 998 offer and the conference date.

The lien work is the most detailed part of a PI settlement, and an easy place for money to slip. The Operator can keep the lien ledger for the matter as tasks in Smokeball: the health-plan, Medi-Cal, Medicare, and ERISA liens, and the provider liens, who holds each, the amount, and the status of each payoff or reduction, and it chases them down. When the case settles, it can feed the settlement statement and the disbursement list. Smokeball runs the trust accounting and the math. The Operator doesn't move trust money. It prepares the numbers for a person to execute.

_How it works: it keeps each lien as a tracked task on the matter, with holder, amount, and status, and a scheduled job chases the open payoffs. For the settlement statement, it reads the gross, fees, costs, and lien figures from the matter and lays out the breakdown and the net, for a person to execute in Smokeball._

## Building your paralegals' competency

You asked for the Operator to help your paralegals grow, and we think that's the right ambition, so it runs through every step rather than sitting off to the side. When the Operator does a piece of work, it can explain it: what the step is, why it matters, what comes next, and when to bring in the attorney. A junior paralegal working alongside it picks up the real California litigation process by doing it with a guide present, instead of learning it the hard way.

_How it works: it's built into each routine's output. When the Operator prepares a step, it can include a short note on what it did, the rule it applied (for example, the response deadline and where it came from), and when to bring in the attorney, so the reasoning sits next to the work in the matter._
