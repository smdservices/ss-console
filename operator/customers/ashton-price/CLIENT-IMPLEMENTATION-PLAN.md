## How the rollout works

We bring your Operator up in stages, and each stage has to prove itself before
the next one starts. Nothing it produces reaches a client, a court, or another
party on its own, at any point. And nothing touches your Smokeball account until
we have sat down together first: before that session, all of the building and
testing happens on our own systems, never on yours.

**1. A working session with you.** We sit down together and walk through our
picture of how a case moves through your office, from filing to settlement. You
correct it where we have it wrong. In the same session you set the dial: for each
kind of work, whether the Operator handles it or prepares it and brings it to a
person. The dial is yours, and you can change it whenever. You choose which
matters it watches first. And the connection itself happens right there in the
room: you authorize the Operator's access to your Smokeball account on a single
approval screen in your portal, and we confirm together, live, that it sees what
it should and nothing more.

**2. Quiet observation.** The Operator starts watching the matters you chose, and
everything it produces comes to us, not to your team. You will not see anything
yet, and that is deliberate. We compare what it noticed, the deadlines it read,
the items it flagged, against what your team actually did on the same matters,
and we tune it until it is consistently right.

**3. It goes live, one area at a time.** Your confirmation on an area is what
turns it on. Each area starts with the Operator preparing the work and bringing
it to a person, and earns more room only where you have said so. Nothing that
goes to a client or the court leaves without an attorney in front of it.

**4. Steady operation.** Once live, the Operator keeps tuning as it goes. When
someone corrects it, a date, a document type, a draft, it carries that correction
forward. We review its work with you and widen what it handles on its own only
where you have said so.

## What we're bringing up for you

The Operator is built for the whole life of a case: intake and service,
discovery, medical records and the chronology, motions, minor's compromise,
trial prep, and mediation, settlement, and liens. The proposal walks through
each of those; this is the order we bring them up in.

**Discovery comes first**, because you told us it is where the most slips. When
discovery is live, your team receives its work there: client verification
tracked and chased until it is signed, served discovery captured with the
response deadline read off the proof of service and flagged for the attorney to
confirm, responses staged, the separate statement assembled for an attorney to
finalize, and the other side's thin or late responses surfaced with a
meet-and-confer letter drafted for the attorney's decision.

**The rest of the case turns on the same way, matter by matter.** For each of the
other areas we confirm our picture of how you actually run it, then activate it
when a real matter presents the work: a case just filed, medical records
arriving, a motion on calendar, a minor's compromise, a matter heading to trial,
a settlement with liens to clear. Each area earns its place the way discovery
does, and each starts by preparing the work for a person, never acting on its
own.

## How we test before you rely on it

Four layers, in order, and every piece of the Operator's work passes through all
of them before your team leans on it. The first two are already behind us; the
rehearsal report captures what they produced.

1. **Practice documents.** Every routine has been exercised against a large set
   of practice matters and documents, including deliberately tricky ones:
   wrong-matter lookalikes, malformed proofs of service, documents that try to
   mislead it. Done.
2. **A rehearsal office.** We keep our own practice account on Smokeball, the
   same platform your firm runs, filled with matters and documents we wrote
   ourselves; your data never appears there. We ran full chains of work through
   it, served discovery through drafted response and on across the rest of the
   lifecycle, and graded every step. That is the rehearsal the report describes.
   It stays alive for as long as we work together: every change we make proves
   itself there before it goes anywhere near your matters.
3. **Quiet observation on your real matters.** Once connected, it works your
   matters with everything routed to us, not your team. We grade it against what
   your team actually did, and it stays invisible to your team until it is
   consistently right.
4. **Your review.** You see the evidence and confirm each part of the process
   before it starts working with your team. Your confirmation is what turns a
   part on; we never flip that switch for you.

## A Smokeball item we tracked to resolution

Saving memos and documents into Smokeball failed for a stretch because of an
issue on Smokeball's side. We opened a support ticket, kept it warm, and
Smokeball fixed it; we have verified the fix end-to-end in our rehearsal
environment. Tasks and calendar entries were never affected. Nothing in the
rollout waits on this anymore; we will confirm the same fix on your account as
part of the connect step.

## What happens when

We do not run this rollout on a calendar; we run it on proof. Each stage starts
when the one before it has shown it works, and the pace is set by the evidence
and by your availability for the items above, not by dates we would have to guess
at today.
