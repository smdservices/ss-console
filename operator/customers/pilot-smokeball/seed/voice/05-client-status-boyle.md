**What a deposition actually is**

Their lawyer asks you questions. You answer under oath. A court reporter types every word. There is no judge, no jury, and no audience. It will happen in our conference room with six people in it.

It is not a test. You cannot fail it.

The only real risk in a deposition is guessing, and we are going to spend most of Friday on that one habit.

**The three rules**

One. Answer only the question you were asked, then stop talking. Silence after your answer is the oldest technique in the business. Somebody will let three seconds go by hoping you fill them. Let them sit there.

Two. If you do not know, say you do not know. If you do not remember, say you do not remember. Those are complete, correct, truthful answers and they are not weaknesses. The wrong answer is a good faith guess that turns out to be off by a car length or ten miles an hour, because two years from now a lawyer will read your guess back to a jury as though you had lied.

Three. If I say the word "objection," stop talking mid sentence. Nine times out of ten I am making a record and you will answer anyway. Just give me the beat.

**What they are going to ask about**

They will walk you through the collision minute by minute. Where you were going, which lane, when you first saw the truck.

They will ask about your medical treatment, every provider, every gap.

They will ask about the eleven days in March when you did not go to physical therapy. You had the flu and your daughter had the flu. That is the answer. It is a good answer. Say it plainly and do not apologize for it.

They will ask what you cannot do now that you could do before. Take your time with that one. Do not give them the summary version. Tell them about the specific things: the grocery bags, the second flight of stairs at your mother's house, sleeping on your right side.

They will ask about your 2021 shoulder. Yes, you hurt it. Yes, you had an MRI. No, nobody operated, and you were discharged in eight weeks and did not see a doctor for that shoulder again for four years. That is the truth and the truth is good for us.

**What not to do between now and then**

Do not go back and reread your medical records to try to memorize them. You are not supposed to know them. If they hand you a document, you are entitled to read the whole thing, slowly, before you answer anything about it.

Do not post anything. Not about the case, not about your health, not a photograph of a weekend. I know you have heard me say this. I am saying it again three weeks before the deposition because this is exactly when it tends to happen.

Do not talk about the case with anyone other than me, Priya, or your husband.
