Dear Ms. Salcedo:

Your insured's dog had already bitten one person before it bit Micah Tolliver in the face.

We represent Micah through her mother and guardian ad litem, Renata Tolliver. This is her demand.

**What happened**

Micah was seven years old and standing in your insured's front yard on a Saturday morning. She had been invited. Ms. Barre's grandson Cole is her friend and the two of them had been in and out of that yard most of the summer.

Ranger is a seventy eight pound bullmastiff mix. At approximately 10:20 a.m. on September 27, 2025, he pushed through the front screen door and came into the yard. The door latch had been broken since at least July, which Ms. Barre confirmed to Los Angeles County Animal Care Officer J. Mireles during his October 1 investigation.

Ranger knocked Micah down and bit her once across the left side of her face.

Cole's older brother pulled the dog off. Ms. Barre came out and, to her credit, called 911 and rode with them. We are not here to say she is a bad person. We are here because her dog was a known risk and her door did not latch.

**The dog had a history**

On March 11, 2025, Ranger bit a United States Postal Service carrier on the left forearm at the same address. USPS incident report 2025-LKW-0338. The carrier required sutures. The route was suspended for eleven days and Ms. Barre received written notice from the Postmaster.

That was six months and sixteen days before he bit a child in the face.

Under Civil Code section 3342 your insured is strictly liable, because Micah was lawfully on private property and the dog bit her. The strict liability statute makes the prior bite legally unnecessary.

We are telling you about it anyway, because it is the difference between a case about a dog and a case about a decision.

**The injuries**

Two cuts. Neither of them is in a place that hides.

The first runs four centimeters diagonally across her left cheek, from just below the outer corner of her eye toward the corner of her mouth. It went through the full thickness of the skin.

The second is fifteen millimeters along the margin of her lower left eyelid, the edge where the lashes come out. Eyelid margins are unforgiving. Repaired imperfectly they notch, and the notch is what people see.

Dr. Selina Arroyo, plastic surgery, repaired both in the emergency department at Miller Children's and Women's Hospital that afternoon under sedation. Twenty two sutures. The eyelid margin took eight of them under loupe magnification.

Ranger's rabies vaccination was current, so the immunoglobulin protocol was not needed. That is the one piece of luck in this file.

**Where the scar is now, and where it goes**

Micah has completed six sessions of pulsed dye laser and eight months of silicone sheeting and daily sun protection. The cheek scar is now a raised pink line that is visible across a room. The eyelid margin has a two millimeter step at the lash line.

Dr. Arroyo's April 30, 2026 report says what every pediatric plastic surgeon says: you do not do definitive revision on a growing face. The recommendation is surgical scar revision at skeletal maturity, around age fifteen, with a separate lid margin revision. Her written estimate is $22,000, with a second stage at $6,500 if the lid does not take.

That is eight years of a child looking at that line in the mirror before anyone can do anything meaningful about it.

**The part that is not on any imaging**

Micah stopped sleeping through the night for four months. She wet the bed twice, at seven years old, after two years of not doing that. She will not walk on the Cedarcrest side of the street, which means her mother drives her four blocks to a school she used to walk to.

Dr. Nathan Kealoha, a child psychologist in Los Alamitos, has seen her fourteen times. His assessment is post traumatic stress, specific phobia of dogs, improving. He expects further episodic treatment around each surgical stage, which is normal and which we have not priced.
