## IV. DAMAGES

The economic damages in this case are small. We are going to say so first, because the defense brief will say it on page one and we would rather own it than answer it.

**A. Past medical specials: $24,380.**

| Provider                                                  | Amount      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Miller Children's, emergency and plastic surgical repair  | $14,880     |
| Selina Arroyo, M.D., follow up                            | $1,940      |
| Scar therapy, six pulsed dye laser sessions and materials | $4,200      |
| Nathan Kealoha, Psy.D., 14 sessions                       | $3,360      |
| **Total**                                                 | **$24,380** |

Sixty one percent of that is one afternoon in an emergency department with a plastic surgeon and twenty two sutures. There is no inflated chiropractic column here, no lien factory, and no provider in this file that plaintiff's counsel referred her to.

**B. Future medical: $22,000, with a contingent $6,500.**

Dr. Arroyo's April 30, 2026 report recommends surgical revision of the cheek scar and the left lower lid margin at skeletal maturity, which she places at approximately age fifteen. Her written estimate is $22,000. If the lid margin does not take, a second stage runs $6,500.

The defense has deposed Dr. Arroyo and did not challenge the recommendation, the timing, or the price. (Arroyo Depo. 104:3 to 111:22.)

**C. General damages: what this case actually is.**

Micah Tolliver was seven years old. She is now eight.

The injury is a four centimeter scar running diagonally across her left cheek from the outer corner of her eye toward the corner of her mouth, and a fifteen millimeter scar along the margin of her lower left eyelid with a two millimeter step at the lash line. Both are on her face. Neither is under her hair, behind her ear, or anywhere clothing goes.

We have attached clinical photographs at 48 hours, six months, and eight months as Exhibits 11 through 13. We would ask the mediator to look at the eight month photograph before either side says a number, because it is the only piece of evidence in this case that argues for itself.

Three points frame the general damage figure.

_First, the duration._ Micah's life expectancy under the standard table is 72.4 additional years. Definitive revision cannot occur for another seven years, and revision improves a scar. It does not remove one. Dr. Arroyo's testimony was that a realistic post revision outcome is "a fine line that is visible on close inspection in most lighting." (Arroyo Depo. 118:9 to 14.) The defense will characterize that as a good result. It is a good result. It is also a permanent one.

_Second, the location._ The two square inches at issue are the part of a human being that other humans look at first and longest. This is not an abstraction and it is not advocacy: it is why facial scar cases in this county have never valued the way an equivalently sized scar on a forearm values, and why the defense's comparables, all of which involve extremity scarring, are not comparables.

_Third, the psychological injury, which is real and which we have not padded._ Dr. Kealoha diagnosed post traumatic stress disorder and a specific phobia of dogs. Micah stopped sleeping through the night for four months and had two episodes of nocturnal enuresis at age seven after two years without any. She will not walk on the Cedarcrest side of her own street, so her mother now drives her four blocks to a school she used to walk to with Cole Barre.

Dr. Kealoha's prognosis is genuinely good. He expects substantial resolution of the phobia with continued exposure work, and he anticipates only episodic treatment around each surgical stage. We have not priced that future treatment and we are not asking for it. We tell you his prognosis is favorable because it is, and because a plaintiff's brief that reported every symptom as permanent would be worth less to you than one that does not.

**D. The minor's compromise, which sets the floor.**

Any resolution requires court approval under Probate Code section 3500 and California Rule of Court 7.955. A judge will read Dr. Arroyo's report, look at Exhibits 11 through 13, and decide whether the figure is fair to this child before any money moves. Defense counsel and the carrier are not the last readers of this settlement.

That is worth holding in mind at the point in the afternoon when the numbers stop moving.

**E. Plaintiff's evaluation.**

Coverage is $500,000. Liability is strict under Civil Code section 3342, and independently negligent given the March 11, 2025 bite of a USPS carrier at the same address and the broken screen door latch that defendant admitted to Animal Care Officer Mireles.

Plaintiff values this case at $340,000 and comes to mediation prepared to resolve it.
