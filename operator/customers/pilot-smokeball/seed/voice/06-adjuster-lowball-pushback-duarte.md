Dear Ms. Whitcomb:

You offered $47,500.

Ms. Duarte's medical bills to date are $68,561. Your offer does not cover the bills that have already been incurred, before anyone talks about her wrist, her neck, the eleven weeks she did not work, or the fusion her surgeon put in writing.

Rejected.

**Your three stated reasons, in order**

_One. You say the neck is degenerative._

The radiologist's language on the September 30 MRI is "disc extrusion at C5-6 with right paracentral component contacting the exiting C6 nerve root." Not desiccation. Not spondylosis. An extrusion, contacting a nerve root, on the exact side where she has thumb and index finger numbness on physical examination.

Ms. Duarte is thirty four years old, has never had a cervical complaint in her life, has no prior cervical imaging of any kind, and developed right arm symptoms within seventy two hours of being hit in the driver's door by a van.

If your position is that this herniation was there before August 14, you will need a physician to say so under oath. Send me the name and I will notice the deposition.

_Two. You say the treatment gap in October undercuts causation._

We told you about that gap in the demand letter, before you found it, and we told you why. Her mother died. The hospice records are at Tab 12 and the death certificate is enclosed now.

You are welcome to make that argument to twelve people in Norwalk. I would like you to think about how it sounds out loud.

_Three. You say the future surgery is speculative._

Dr. Achebe's January 20 report recommends a single level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion at C5-6, states the indication, and attaches a $96,400 estimate. That is not a maybe. That is a board certified spine surgeon's written treatment plan after nine months of failed conservative care, thirty four physical therapy visits, and two epidural injections that gave her nine days.

If you want it to stop being speculative, she will schedule it. Then it will be a bill instead of an estimate, and the demand will be higher, not lower.

**What you have not disputed**

You have not disputed liability. You have not disputed that your driver ran the light, that he was cited, that he was on the clock in a company van, or that an independent witness watched him do it.

So we are only arguing about damages, and your own file already concedes $68,561 of them.

**The number**

The demand remains $385,000.

I will tell you plainly where I think this lands, because I would rather have a short negotiation than a long one. This case settles in the mid two hundreds with a reasonable adjuster and a mediator. It does not settle at $47,500, and it will not, and the four weeks between now and my filing deadline are the cheapest four weeks this file will ever have.

**What happens April 3**

We file in Los Angeles Superior Court on April 3.

At that point Copperline gets served, Copperline's risk manager reads a complaint that describes a driver who ran a red light eleven minutes into his shift, and your defense counsel starts billing. The maintenance file, the route logs, the telematics from that van, and Mr. Brennan's prior moving violations all become discoverable, and I will ask for every one of them.

I am also enclosing a copy of this letter and your March 24 offer for your claim file, because the standard under Insurance Code section 790.03(h) and Egan is what a reasonable insurer does when liability is clear and the special damages exceed the offer.

We would rather resolve this. We are prepared not to.
