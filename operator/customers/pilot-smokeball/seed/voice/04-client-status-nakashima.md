The demand went out May 12. Bayline has it. Here is where things actually stand.

**What we sent them**

We asked for $310,000. That number is built on $71,705 in medical bills and $29,712 in lost income, and then everything else is for the knee itself: the surgery, the four weeks you could not put weight on that leg, and the fact that it does not straighten and will not.

I want you to know that those four pictures are the strongest thing in your file. When we get to a mediator or a jury, that is what they are going to look at.

**What happens next, and when**

Adjusters do not respond in thirty days, whatever the letter says. Expect one of three things by early July.

They may come back with a low number. That is the most likely outcome and it is not a bad sign. First offers on premises cases are usually somewhere between fifteen and thirty percent of the demand, and it means an adjuster has read the file and asked for authority.

They may ask for more records. Usually that is real. Sometimes it is a delay.

They may say nothing. If we hit July 15 with silence, we file. I would rather file than be strung out through the summer while your right to sue quietly ages.

**The lien question, which is the one that will matter to you**

Your health plan paid a portion of these bills, and when this case resolves the plan has a right to be paid back out of your settlement. That is called a lien. Nobody explains it to people until the end, and by then it feels like a trick, so I am explaining it now.

The gross number is not your number. Out of any settlement comes the attorney fee, the case costs, and the liens. What is left is yours.

Here is the part that is genuinely good news. Those liens are negotiable, and we negotiate them hard and always after the settlement is agreed, never before. On a case with your bill profile I would expect to reduce the health plan's claim materially, and every dollar I take off that lien goes to you and not to us. Marcus is already pulling the itemization so we are ready to argue it the day we have a number.

**One thing to keep doing**

Keep going to Dr. Guillen for the annual check on that knee even though you feel done with doctors. Not for the case. Because the extensor lag is permanent and somebody should be watching the other knee, which is now doing more work than it was built to do.

And if the district puts anything in writing about the shop assignment for 2027, send it to me the day you get it.

You asked whether this would be done before the school year starts. Honestly, probably not. If they pay, we are looking at late summer or early fall. If we file, we are looking at a year to eighteen months, and I do not want to soften that for you.

I am not going to take a fast number to make a date. The knee is permanent. The offer has to reflect that.

There is nothing you need to do. I will call you myself the day Bayline responds, whether the news is good or bad.
