**What happened**

Cornerstone Market #218 sits at 1120 Bellflower Boulevard in Long Beach. Aisle 7 is dry goods and cooking oils, polished light gray vinyl tile under fluorescent light.

At 5:41 p.m. on November 6, 2025, a 48 ounce bottle of vegetable oil fell from the middle shelf on the east side of the aisle and split. Your camera 4 caught it. Nobody was near it when it fell.

At 5:49 p.m., a Cornerstone employee in a store apron walked north through aisle 7, past the spill, carrying a price gun. Camera 4 caught that too. He did not stop.

At 6:02 p.m., the same employee walked south through aisle 7, past the spill again. He did not stop that time either. We have since identified him as Devon Pryce.

At 6:15 p.m., Mr. Nakashima turned into aisle 7 carrying a twenty pound bag of dog food against his chest. He went down hard on his left leg.

No cone. No mat. No absorbent. No employee. Nothing between 5:41 and 6:15 except two chances your own staff had and did not take.

Your insured's sweep log for that evening shows a sweep entry at 5:30 p.m. and the next at 6:30 p.m. That is a one hour interval on a Thursday evening in an oil aisle. Their own written policy, produced in your claim file at page 41, requires thirty minute sweeps in aisles 6 through 9.

**The injuries**

The big muscle on the front of Mr. Nakashima's thigh tore completely away from the top of his kneecap (full thickness left quadriceps tendon rupture). When that happens the leg simply stops holding. He could not stand up in the aisle and he could not lift his own foot off the floor.

Dr. Anthony Guillen at Pacific Coast Orthopaedic repaired it surgically on November 12 with suture anchors through drill tunnels in the patella. Mr. Nakashima was non weight bearing for four weeks and in a locked brace for six. Physical therapy ran from December 22 through May 4, forty two visits.

He has a permanent ten degree extensor lag. In plain terms, his left leg will not go completely straight anymore, and it never will. Dr. Guillen's May 4 discharge note says so.

He cannot kneel.

**Why that sentence matters**

Errol Nakashima has taught woodshop at Lakewood High School for twenty six years. Almost all of that job is done on a knee: at a jointer, at a bandsaw table, at floor level next to a student who is about to feed a board the wrong direction.

He is fifty eight. He had intended to work another nine years. He has now filed for a partial accommodation and has been told the district cannot guarantee a shop assignment past the 2027 school year.

**What you are going to say, and the answer**

You are going to say the oil was open and obvious.

Vegetable oil on light gray tile is clear. It has no edge. It does not look like anything until you are in it. Mr. Nakashima was carrying a bag of dog food at chest height, which is exactly how a store designs you to carry a bag of dog food.

And your own employee walked past that spill twice, at 5:49 and at 6:02, sober, unencumbered, on the clock, and paid to look at that floor. If it was open and obvious, he had two chances to see it and he did not see it either.

You cannot have that argument both ways.
