## II. STATEMENT OF FACTS

Your Honor, this case has a video, and the video has four timestamps. We would ask you to start there, because everything else in this brief is downstream of them.

**5:41 p.m.** A 48 ounce bottle of vegetable oil falls from the middle shelf on the east side of aisle 7 at Cornerstone Market #218, 1120 Bellflower Boulevard, Long Beach, and splits open. Camera 4 records it. No customer is within twelve feet. Nobody knocked it off. Defendant has never contended otherwise.

**5:49 p.m.** Cornerstone employee Devon Pryce walks northbound through aisle 7 carrying a handheld price gun. He passes within three feet of the spill. He does not stop, does not report it, and does not place a cone. Mr. Pryce testified at his April 22, 2026 deposition that he "didn't see anything" and that he was "working the shelf tags." (Pryce Depo. 61:8 to 62:4.)

**6:02 p.m.** Mr. Pryce walks southbound through aisle 7 and passes the spill a second time. He again does not stop. (Pryce Depo. 66:19 to 67:11.)

**6:15 p.m.** Plaintiff Errol Nakashima turns into aisle 7 carrying a twenty pound bag of dog food held against his chest. He steps into the oil and his left leg gives out beneath him.

Thirty four minutes elapsed between the hazard and the fall. During thirty four minutes, in a supermarket aisle stocked with cooking oil, on a Thursday evening, an employee of the defendant walked past the spill twice.

**A. Defendant's own policy required a sweep every thirty minutes.**

Cornerstone's written Store Safety Manual, produced as CM 000388 to 000391, requires documented floor sweeps at thirty minute intervals in aisles 6 through 9, designated "high risk aisles" because of liquid product. The manual identifies cooking oil by name.

The sweep log for November 6, 2025, produced as CM 000412, records a sweep at 5:30 p.m. and the next at 6:30 p.m. That is a sixty minute interval in a thirty minute aisle. Store Director Yvette Camarillo confirmed at deposition that the 6:00 p.m. sweep was not performed and that she did not know why. (Camarillo Depo. 88:2 to 89:20.)

**B. Notice is not in dispute, and defendant has stopped pretending it is.**

Defendant's answer pleaded lack of notice. Defendant's own video establishes actual notice through Mr. Pryce at 5:49 p.m., twenty six minutes before the fall.

This is not an _Ortega_ case. We are not asking a trier of fact to infer duration from the condition of the substance. The duration is on the recording, in the corner of the frame, with the time on it.

**C. The comparative fault defense, taken at its highest.**

Defendant contends the spill was open and obvious and that Mr. Nakashima should have seen it.

We concede the facts underlying that argument and dispute what they mean. Mr. Nakashima was carrying a bag of dog food at chest height, which obstructed his view of the floor immediately in front of him. He was not looking at the ground. He was looking down the aisle for the shelf tag.

Three things answer it.

First, refined vegetable oil is colorless and has no discernible edge on the polished light gray vinyl tile Cornerstone installed in that aisle. Plaintiff's retained safety expert, Dr. Halden Oyelaran, measured the coefficient of friction of that tile wet at 0.31, below the 0.42 threshold Cornerstone's own flooring specification calls for.

Second, a market that stacks dog food in fifty pound and twenty pound bags at the end of aisle 6 has designed the very carry position it now says was careless.

Third, and this is the point we expect to spend the day on: Mr. Pryce walked past that spill twice, unencumbered, sober, on the clock, and specifically paid to look at that floor. He did not see it either. Defendant cannot argue that a hazard was obvious to a shopper carrying a bag of dog food and simultaneously invisible to the employee who passed it twice with his hands free.

**D. What defendant has never disputed.**

Defendant has never disputed that the oil was its product, that it fell from its shelf, that its employee passed it twice, that its sweep policy was violated, or that Mr. Nakashima's quadriceps tendon ruptured in that aisle.

Liability in this case is not close. We say that plainly at the outset so that the day can be spent on the number instead of on a fight both sides know how it ends.
