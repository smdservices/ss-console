Dear Mr. Ansel:

We are not able to represent you, and I want to explain why in enough detail that you can do something about it.

**The problem is a deadline, not your injuries**

You fell on an interior stairwell of the Marina Vista Parking Structure on May 4, 2025, and broke your left ankle in three places. I do not doubt that. The records you brought are consistent with everything you told us, and the photograph of the broken handrail bracket is a real piece of evidence.

The Marina Vista structure is owned and operated by the City of Long Beach. That is what changes everything here.

When the party responsible is a city, a county, a school district, or any other public entity, California law does not give you two years to sue. It gives you six months to file a written claim with that entity before you may sue at all. That is Government Code section 911.2. Your six months ran out on November 4, 2025.

No claim was filed. That is not a criticism of you. Almost nobody outside this business knows that rule exists, and the reason it exists is to protect public entities, not you.

**There is one door left and it is closing**

There is a procedure to ask a public entity, and then a court, for permission to file a late claim. It is Government Code sections 911.4 and 946.6, and it requires an application to the entity within **one year of the incident**.

One year from May 4, 2025 was May 4, 2026.

That date has passed. I am not going to tell you the door is definitively shut, because there are narrow arguments about when a claim accrues and about the delayed discovery of an injury or of a defendant's identity, and a lawyer who has your complete file and more time than we have had may see something we did not.

But you should assume you are in serious trouble on timing and act like it.

**What I want you to do this week**

Take this letter and your entire file to another attorney immediately. Not next month. This week.

Bring the emergency room records, the surgical report from Dr. Whitfield, the photograph of the bracket, and any letter, email, or claim form you sent to anyone at the City, even if you are not sure it counted. If you spoke to a City risk employee by phone at any point, write down the date and the name before you forget it.

**Two things I have to say clearly**

We have not been your lawyers at any point, we have not filed anything for you, and we are not filing anything for you. No attorney client relationship was formed by your consultation or by this letter.

We have not fully investigated your case and our decision is not an opinion that your claim has no value. We reviewed the timing, the ownership of the structure, and the claim requirement, and that was as far as we went. Another attorney could reach a different conclusion, and you are entitled to that second opinion.

Separately, the ordinary deadline to sue a private party for a personal injury in California is two years, which for you would be May 4, 2027. If any private company maintained that stairwell under contract with the City, that is a different defendant with a different deadline, and it is worth someone looking at hard and soon.
