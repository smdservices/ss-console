## How the rollout works

We bring your Operator up in stages, and each stage has to prove itself before
the next one starts. Nothing it produces reaches a client, a court, or another
party on its own, at any point. And nothing touches the systems you run until we
have sat down together first: before that session, all of the building and
testing happens on our own systems, never on yours. The stages are the same for
every Operator we stand up. What changes is the work we point it at, which is
yours to define.

**1. A working session with you.** We sit down together and walk through our
picture of how the work moves through your office. You correct it where we have
it wrong. In the same session you set the dial: for each kind of work, whether
the Operator handles it or prepares it and brings it to a person. The dial is
yours, and you can change it whenever. You choose what it watches first. And the
connection itself happens right there in the room: you authorize the Operator's
access on a single approval screen in your portal, and we confirm together,
live, that it sees what it should and nothing more.

**2. Quiet observation.** The Operator starts watching what you chose, and
everything it produces comes to us, not to your team. You will not see anything
yet, and that is deliberate. We compare what it noticed against what your team
actually did on the same work, and we tune it until it is consistently right.

**3. It goes live, one area at a time.** Your confirmation on an area is what
turns it on. Each area starts with the Operator preparing the work and bringing
it to a person, and earns more room only where you have said so. Nothing that
leaves your office goes out on its own; a person is always in front of it. Areas
turn on in the order that helps you most, and each one earns its place the way
the first did.

**4. Steady operation.** Once live, the Operator keeps tuning as it goes. When
someone corrects it, a date, a document type, a draft, it carries that
correction forward. We review its work with you and widen what it handles on its
own only where you have said so.

## How we test before you rely on it

Four layers, in order, and every piece of the Operator's work passes through all
of them before your team leans on it:

1. **Practice documents.** Every routine is exercised against a large set of
   practice items, including deliberately tricky ones: lookalikes that belong to
   the wrong file, malformed inputs, documents that try to mislead it.
2. **A rehearsal office.** We keep our own practice account on the same platform
   you run, filled with material we write ourselves; your data never appears
   there. Full chains of work run in that account before anything touches yours,
   and every change we make, for as long as we work together, goes through the
   rehearsal office first.
3. **Quiet observation on your real work.** As above: it works, we grade it
   against what your team actually did, and it stays invisible to your team until
   it is consistently right.
4. **Your review.** You see the evidence and confirm each area before it starts
   working with your team. Your confirmation is what turns an area on; we never
   flip that switch for you.

## What happens when

We do not run this rollout on a calendar; we run it on proof. Each stage starts
when the one before it has shown it works, and the pace is set by the evidence
and by your availability for what only you can give, not by dates we would have
to guess at today.
